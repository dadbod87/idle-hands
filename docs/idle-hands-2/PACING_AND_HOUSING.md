# Slow progression, personal homes and cooperative depth

User direction recorded 2026-11-09; numbers below are agent-selected design targets to simulate, not implemented timers or final prices.

## Pace without busywork

Teach walking/interaction in a minute and one craft/place/contribution in five. After that, make stages worth anticipating: a useful workshop improvement in 1–3 ordinary days; first house plot/starting house around 1–2 weeks; meaningful home upgrades and town unlocks over 3–6 weeks; neighborhood expansion over months. Validate cohorts and resource paths before committing prices. “Days” means a reasonable pattern of return visits, not continuous clicking or a requirement to stay logged in.

Use a small number of understandable material flows and visible production jobs. Keep active gathering bounded by resource availability and carrying/storage capacity, with useful alternate activities while things develop. No infinite click-to-currency button, exponential staff compounding, paid acceleration, energy purchases or artificial overnight penalties. Test 10-minute/day idle, 30-minute/day regular and 2-hour/day active cohorts; active players should have more choices/exploration and a modest early lead, not finish six weeks in an afternoon. Initial tuning target: an active cohort reaches major economy gates no more than roughly twice as fast as the regular cohort; this is a simulation goal, not a promise or a forced hidden cap.

Avoid long timers as the only source of depth. Recipe dependencies, tool choices, shared construction stages and optional errands should create decisions. Show next goals and exact prerequisites; never conceal a time gate behind unclear pricing. No missed-day streak penalty. Idle jobs settle from server timestamps with explicit storage/job limits; weekly objectives remain separate from permanent assets.

## Teamwork and humor

Shared projects need useful work for both newcomers and established players: several material contributions, transparent totals and receipts, visible stages and a shared new destination. Do not make one rich player bypass all cooperation; simulate contribution distribution and alternate routes without requiring one specific absent person. Individual materials remain private until donated, no unilateral demolition, and newcomers can contribute without prior wealth. Offline members retain their property.

Humor lives in NPC conversations, descriptions, evolving signs and project stages—not only random popups. Example: bridge repair progresses from “committee formed” to “wood located” to “ribbon budget denied.” Leave useful information legible under the joke. No real private coworker details, offensive targeting or reward for harassing people.

## A home of your own

Start in a rented/shared workshop. Plot acquisition is a deliberate early long-term goal using earned resources and a clear ownership transaction. The first district must have expandable/instanced plots so invite order cannot permanently lock later players out. Address/plot ID is distinct from cosmetic house style. Each player's home is a separate ownership boundary, not a shared room anyone can edit.

Stages: claim plot → modest starter home → expanded floor plan/rooms → exterior and interior variations → earned decorative collections. Upgrades preserve existing decoration/finishes and provide a valid placement migration or preview if footprints change. Visitors may inspect; edits require owner permission. No stealing, eviction or public demolition in the first release. Weekly score changes never touch plots, upgrades or decoration. Custom colors preserve each item's silhouette and tier identity.

IH2-010 only labels/inspects a future home plot. IH2-030 prototypes placement locally; IH2-040 proves ownership and safe transactions; IH2-050 implements a modest plot/starter-home upgrade/decoration path with synthetic progression-time controls for QA. Full housing depth expands after beta. No fake purchase control in a prototype that has no server.

## Evidence before balancing

Add a deterministic progression simulator before final economy numbers: cohort schedules, recipes, resource sources/sinks, storage/idle caps, project completion, newcomer usefulness and housing milestones. Replay at least six weeks plus absence/rejoin. Report ranges and bottlenecks. Long-duration play is simulated only in isolated fixtures, never by granting or changing real accounts. Reopen price/timer assumptions if any cohort races through or becomes stuck.
